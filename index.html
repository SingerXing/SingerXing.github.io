UW–Madison MXM · ResearchFeb – May 2026

Learning statistically accurate dynamics of complex systems

Built an autoencoder–LSTM model in PyTorch that compresses a chaotic 40-variable system to a 13-dimensional state and forecasts it, then extended it to learn from statistical observations like regional averages and Fourier modes.

40 → 13dimensions after compression
2systems: Lorenz–96 and FitzHugh–Nagumo
4researchers, equal contribution
PyTorchNumPyAutoencodersLSTMData assimilation
Read the case study

The question. Real observations of complex systems are often summaries, like averages or variances, and many states share the same summary. Can a compact learned model be corrected by those statistics and still recover the full physical state?

What we built. A convolutional encoder with circular padding (the system lives on a ring) maps 40 sites to 8 learned coordinates. Five observed values are appended. An LSTM with two layers forecasts in that 13-dimensional space, and the decoder maps forecasts back to all 40 sites. The figure shows reconstruction tracking variables that are never observed directly.

Extensions. We replaced point observations with block averages and Fourier coefficients, tested two ways to couple them to the learned coordinates, and ran forecasts on the FitzHugh–Nagumo spiking model.

The ensemble Kalman filter correction is a proposed design, not a finished result. Team: Daniel Youngberg, Tan Bui, Cody McKenna and me.

STEALTH Ventures · Quantitative AnalysisAug – Sep 2026

Grid vs. random vs. Bayesian tuning, compared fairly

Built a TF-IDF + LinearSVC text classifier, then rebuilt the tuning benchmark after mentor feedback so all three search strategies got the same compute budget, three random seeds, and a sensitivity analysis.

288trials, 1,440 CV model fits
0.774best CV macro F1 (Bayesian)
<0.002spread between strategies' mean best
scikit-learnRay TuneHyperOptNLPTransformers
Read the case study

Feedback loop. My first version ran all three strategies side by side. My mentor noted that wasn't a fair runtime comparison and asked which hyperparameters mattered. The revision fixed concurrency at one CPU and one trial, rotated strategy order, recorded real completion times, and added paired contrasts over the full grid.

Finding. On this small search space the three strategies finished within 0.002 CV F1 of each other. Bayesian search found the best single run but varied most across seeds. Against the untuned baseline (test F1 0.7364), the CV-selected model scored 0.7428.

Also for STEALTH. A logistic regression on breast-cancer data, where nested cross-validation showed tuning gave no real advantage, which I reported alongside the higher held-out accuracy. And a transformer fine-tuned on startup and investment text.

Models were selected by cross-validation, never by test score. Three seeds don't support claiming one strategy is best in general.
